Chapter 6 · National Economic Accounts

GDP accounting provides the scoreboard for macro policy. Know the definitions, the three measurement approaches, and what stays out of the tally.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

  • Definition: Market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a given period.
  • Measured quarterly and annualized for consistency.
  • Final goods only: Intermediate inputs excluded to avoid double counting.

Three Approaches

Expenditure: GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)
Income: GDP = Wages + Rent + Interest + Profit (WRIP)
Value-Added: Sum of (Sales − Intermediate Inputs) across firms

All three yield the same total because every dollar spent becomes someone else's income.

Real vs Nominal GDP

Real GDP = (Nominal GDP × 100) / Price Index
  • Nominal: Current prices.
  • Real: Constant base-year prices; removes inflation.
  • GDP Deflator: Broad price index covering all domestically produced goods and services.

What GDP Leaves Out

  • Underground/illegal markets.
  • Non-market household production.
  • Intermediate goods (counted in final goods).
  • Secondhand sales and purely financial transactions (stocks, bonds).

Example: Selling tomatoes to a pickle factory counts only when the pickles are sold to the final consumer.

Beyond GDP

  • GDP per capita: GDP ÷ population; proxy for average living standards.
  • Gross National Product (GNP): GDP + net factor income from abroad.
  • National Income (NI): GNP − depreciation − indirect business taxes.
