Chapter 14 · Quantity Theory of Money

Classical economists summarized the long-run link between money and prices with a simple identity: MV = PY.

Equation of Exchange

M × V = P × Y
  • M: Money supply
  • V: Velocity of money (average times each dollar is spent)
  • P: Price level
  • Y: Real output

If V is stable and Y is pinned to potential output, changes in M translate directly into proportional changes in P.

Quantity Theory Conclusions

  • Excessive money growth causes inflation. Hyperinflation = money supply exploding faster than output.
  • Monetary neutrality: In the long run, money affects nominal variables (prices, wages) but not real variables (output, employment).
  • Policy implication: Keep money growth aligned with real GDP growth to stabilize prices.

Classical View vs Modern Nuance

  • Classical: Prices and wages are flexible → economy gravitates to full employment quickly; "money doesn’t matter" for real output.
  • Modern: In the short run, sticky prices mean monetary policy can affect real GDP; long-run neutrality still holds.
