Chapter 13 · Fed Policy Tools & Interest Rate Targeting

The Federal Reserve manipulates the money supply and the price of reserves to guide interest rates. Know every lever.

Main Policy Tools

Open Market Operations (OMO)

Buying government securities injects reserves (MS ↑, interest rates ↓). Selling securities drains reserves (MS ↓, rates ↑).

Discount Rate

The rate the Fed charges banks for loans from the discount window. Lower DR → easier borrowing; higher DR → signals restraint.

Federal Funds Rate

Market rate banks charge each other for overnight reserves. The Fed targets this rate through OMOs.

Interest on Reserves (IOR)

Paying more on reserves encourages banks to hold them, reducing lending. Cutting IOR pushes funds into loans.

Reserve Supply & Demand

  • Reserve supply: Determined by the Fed (via OMOs) plus banks' willingness to hold excess reserves.
  • Reserve demand: Driven by required reserves and payment needs.
  • Targets: In a limited-reserves regime, the Fed adjusts reserve supply to hit the funds rate; in an ample-reserves regime, IOR acts as the floor.

Quantity vs Interest-Rate Rules

  • Quantity-based policy: Change the money supply and let interest rates adjust.
  • Interest-rate-based policy: Target a specific rate (federal funds) and adjust the supply of reserves accordingly.

Policy Effectiveness

During deep recessions, monetary policy may be less effective than fiscal policy if banks hoard reserves or if demand for loans is weak. Recognize these limitations on FRQs.
