Chapter 16 · Business Cycles & the AD–AS Model

Aggregate demand and supply explain recessions, inflation, and policy gaps. Master the determinants to predict directional shifts.

Business Cycle Language

  • Peak → Recession → Trough → Expansion: No fixed timetable; cycles vary in length and severity.
  • Output gaps: Recessionary (actual < potential) vs inflationary (actual > potential).

Aggregate Demand (AD)

AD is total spending by households, firms, government, and foreigners at each price level.

Why AD Slopes Down

  • Interest-rate effect
  • Wealth effect
  • Foreign purchases effect

Major Shifters

  • Consumption (income, wealth, expectations, taxes)
  • Investment (interest rates, expectations, tech)
  • Government spending
  • Net exports (foreign income, exchange rates, tastes)

Aggregate Supply

  • Short run (SRAS): Upward sloping due to sticky wages/prices.
  • Shifters: Input prices (wages, commodities), productivity, expectations, taxes/regulations.
  • Wage–price spiral: Rising expected prices push SRAS left as workers negotiate higher wages.

Long-Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS)

Vertical at potential output (full employment). Moves only with changes in resources, technology, or institutions.
