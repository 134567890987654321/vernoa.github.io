Chapter 19 · Savings Identity, Consumption & Fiscal Foundations

Tie together national saving, the consumption function, and the fiscal tools that shift aggregate demand.

Closed Economy Savings Identity

S = (Y − T − C) + (T − G)
  • Private saving: Y − T − C
  • Public saving: T − G (surplus if positive, deficit if negative)
  • National saving: Finances domestic investment (I) when the economy is closed.

Disposable Income & Consumption

  • Disposable income: Yd = Y − T + transfers.
  • Consumption function: C = C0 + MPC × Yd (C0 = autonomous consumption).
  • MPC + MPS = 1: Marginal propensities describe how extra income gets split between spending and saving.

Investment Decision Rule

Businesses invest when the expected real rate of return exceeds the real interest rate. Higher real rates choke off investment demand.

Fiscal Policy Overview

  • Definition: Government decisions about spending (G) and net taxes (T).
  • Expansionary: Raise G or cut T to close a recessionary gap.
  • Contractionary: Cut G or raise T to cool an inflationary gap.

See the dedicated Fiscal Policy Cheat Sheet for deeper coverage.

Multipliers

Spending Multiplier = 1 / (1 − MPC)
Tax Multiplier = −MPC / (1 − MPC)

Net export and balanced-budget multipliers use the same logic. Crowding out in the loanable funds market reduces these theoretical values.

Automatic Stabilizers

  • Progressive taxes fall during recessions, rise during booms.
  • Transfer payments (unemployment insurance, welfare) move opposite the cycle.
  • These features cushion downturns without new legislation.
