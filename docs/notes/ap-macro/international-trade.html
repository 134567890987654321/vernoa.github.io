Chapter 20 · International Trade & Finance

Cap the course with trade gains, balance-of-payments accounting, and how exchange rates move.

Specialization & Terms of Trade

  • Specialization raises world output when each country produces according to comparative advantage.
  • Terms of trade: Must fall between both countries' opportunity costs for both parties to benefit.

Protectionism Effects

  • Tariffs/quotas create deadweight loss.
  • Tariffs transfer revenue to domestic government.
  • Arguments for protection (infant industry, national security) must be weighed against efficiency losses.

Balance of Payments (BOP)

Identity

Current Account + Financial (Capital) Account = 0.

Current Account

Net exports, net investment income, net transfers.

Financial Account

Capital inflows/outflows, including foreign direct investment.

Exchange Rates

  • Appreciation: Currency buys more foreign currency; hurts exports, helps importers.
  • Depreciation: Currency buys less; boosts exports, raises import prices.
  • Determinants: Relative incomes, inflation rates, interest rates, tastes, trade policies.

Exchange Rate Regimes

  • Floating: Market forces set the rate.
  • Fixed/Pegged: Government commits to a specific rate range; requires intervention.
  • Managed float: Mostly market-driven with occasional central bank action.

Purchasing Power Parity & Capital Flows

  • PPP: In the long run, identical goods should cost the same in different countries once exchange rates adjust.
  • Capital inflow: Foreigners buy domestic assets or lend funds domestically.
  • Capital outflow: Domestic savers purchase foreign assets.
