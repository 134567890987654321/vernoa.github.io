Chapter 11 · Money, Aggregates & the Fed

Know what counts as "money," how the monetary aggregates are constructed, and why the Federal Reserve sits at the center of it all.

What Is Money?

  • Medium of exchange: Accepted in payment for goods and services.
  • Unit of account: Provides a common measure of value.
  • Store of value: Transfers purchasing power over time.

Money examples: Currency, demand deposits, transaction accounts, money market mutual funds, savings deposits, small time deposits.

Non-money: Stocks, bonds, real estate, credit cards (they represent borrowing).

Monetary Aggregates

Aggregate Components
Monetary Base (M0) Currency in circulation + bank reserves at the Fed.
M1 Currency + demand deposits + other checkable deposits + traveler's checks.
M2 M1 + savings deposits + small (<$100k) time deposits + retail money market funds.
M3 M2 + large time deposits (>$100k) + institutional money market funds.

Liquidity: M1 is most liquid; as you add savings and time deposits, liquidity falls.

Fiat Money & the Federal Reserve

  • Fiat money: No intrinsic value; accepted because the government says so.
  • Federal Reserve: Central bank of the U.S.—the "bankers' bank."
  • Banks hold reserves at the Fed and can borrow through its lending facilities.
