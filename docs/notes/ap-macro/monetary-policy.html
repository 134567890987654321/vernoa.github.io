Chapter 18 · Monetary Policy: Theory vs Practice

The Fed is the only institution that sets U.S. monetary policy. Learn how changes in the money supply ripple through interest rates, investment, and aggregate demand.

Definition & Goals

  • Monetary policy: Fed actions that change the money supply and interest rates.
  • Goals: Maximum employment, stable prices, moderate long-term interest rates.

Transmission Chain

MS ↑ → Interest rates ↓ → Borrowing/Investment ↑ → AD ↑ → Output gap closes
MS ↓ → Interest rates ↑ → Borrowing/Investment ↓ → AD ↓ → Cools an overheating economy

In diagrams, show AD shifting right for expansionary policy and left for contractionary policy.

Gap Playbook

  • Recessionary gap: Increase MS (buy bonds, cut discount rate, lower reserve requirements, reduce IOR).
  • Inflationary gap: Decrease MS (sell bonds, raise discount rate, raise RR, increase IOR).
  • Watch inventories: Rising inventories signal insufficient AD.

Practice vs Theory

  • In theory: Fed picks a money supply growth path to hit desired AD.
  • In practice: Fed targets the federal funds rate and uses OMOs plus interest on reserves to steer it.
  • Four tools revisited: OMO, discount lending, reserve requirements, interest on reserves.
