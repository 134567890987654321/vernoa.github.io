Chapter 7 · CPI, Inflation & Its Costs

Inflation—along with unemployment—is one of the "twin evils." Track it with the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and understand who gains, who loses, and why high inflation distorts decisions.

The CPI Framework

  • Definition: Measures average price changes for a market basket of goods/services purchased by urban consumers.
  • Steps: Fix the basket → Determine prices → Compute total cost → Select base year → Calculate the index.
  • Use: Converts nominal values into real terms and tracks inflation trends.

Inflation Rate

Inflation = ((CPIcurrent − CPIprevious) / CPIprevious) × 100

AP free-response often asks you to compute CPI-based inflation across two years.

CPI Biases

New Goods

Basket updates slowly, so it misses the value consumers get from new product categories.

Quality Changes

Bettter products cost more but also deliver more value; CPI can overstate inflation when quality improves.

Costs of Inflation

  • Menu costs: Firms must reprint menus/catalogs more often.
  • Misallocation: Relative prices get noisy, causing resources to move toward less-productive uses.
  • Inflation tax: Unexpected inflation transfers wealth from lenders to borrowers.
  • Uncertainty: Harder to plan long-term contracts or investments.

Nominal vs Real Interest Rates

Real interest rate ≈ Nominal interest rate − Inflation rate

Lenders care about real purchasing power, not the sticker rate. Unexpected inflation hurts lenders and helps borrowers.
