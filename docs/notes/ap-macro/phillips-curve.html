Chapter 9 · Phillips Curve & AD–AS Connections

Short-run Phillips Curve (SRPC) visuals mirror the AD–AS model: anything that changes unemployment through aggregate demand shows up as movement along or shifts of the curve.

Short-Run Phillips Curve (SRPC)

  • Downward sloping: Lower unemployment comes with higher inflation in the short run.
  • Movements along: Triggered by AD shifts. Example: Expansionary AD → lower unemployment, higher inflation (movement up-left along SRPC).
  • Shifts: Changes in expected inflation (SRAS shifts) shift the SRPC.

Long-Run Phillips Curve (LRPC)

Vertical at the natural rate of unemployment (NRU). In the long run, inflation expectations adjust so there is no tradeoff.

AD–AS Storyboard

  • AD shifts right: Price level ↑, real GDP ↑, unemployment ↓ → move up-left along SRPC.
  • SRAS shifts right: Price level ↓, real GDP ↑, unemployment ↓ → SRPC shifts downward.
  • Expected prices ↑: SRAS shifts left, SRPC shifts upward (wage-price spiral risk).

Wage–Price Spirals

When workers expect future inflation, they demand higher wages → firms raise prices → expectations become reality. Shows up as SRAS left shift and SRPC upward shift.

Policy Takeaways

  • Demand-pull inflation: Caused by AD increases; temporary unemployment tradeoff.
  • Supply shocks: Shift SRAS left, move SRPC up/right; policy must decide whether to fight inflation or unemployment.
  • Credible policy: Anchors expectations, keeping SRAS and SRPC stable.
