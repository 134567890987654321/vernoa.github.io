Chapter 5 · Demand & Supply Foundations

Micro tools drive macro intuition. Understand how price signals coordinate buyers and sellers to predict everything from shortages to policy impacts.

Demand Basics

  • Definition: Quantity of a good/service households are willing and able to purchase at various prices.
  • Law of Demand: Price ↑ → Quantity demanded ↓ (inverse relationship).
  • Reasons: Income effect (real purchasing power falls) and diminishing marginal utility (each extra unit yields less satisfaction).

Shifts vs Movements (Demand)

Movement Along

Only price changes. Example: A higher price for coffee moves you along the existing demand curve.

Shift of Demand

Non-price factors change demand at every price: income, tastes, expectations, number of buyers, prices of substitutes/complements.

Supply Basics

  • Definition: Quantity firms are willing and able to sell at various prices.
  • Law of Supply: Price ↑ → Quantity supplied ↑ (direct relationship).
  • Key Determinants: Resource/input prices, opportunities in other markets, taxes/subsidies, technology, expectations, number of sellers.

Market Equilibrium

Where demand equals supply.

Surplus: Price above equilibrium → Qs > Qd → downward pressure on price.

Shortage: Price below equilibrium → Qd > Qs → upward pressure on price.

Any shift in demand or supply creates a new equilibrium price and quantity.

Related Markets & Elasticities

  • Substitutes: Goods used in place of each other (coffee vs tea). Price of substitute ↑ → demand shifts right.
  • Complements: Goods consumed together (phones & data plans). Price of complement ↑ → demand shifts left.
  • Elasticity check: Steeper curves → larger price swings, flatter curves → larger quantity swings.
