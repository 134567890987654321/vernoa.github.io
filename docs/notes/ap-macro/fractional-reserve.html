Chapter 12 · Fractional Reserve Banking & the Money Multiplier

Banks keep only a fraction of deposits on hand. The rest cycles into loans, deposits, and new money—unless leakages slow the process.

Key Definitions

  • Required Reserves (RR): Minimum deposits banks must hold to meet withdrawals.
  • Excess Reserves (ER): Deposits above the requirement; fuel new loans.
  • Fractional Reserve System: Allows banks to lend more than the reserves they hold.

T-Accounts

Assets

  • Reserves
  • Loans
  • Securities

Liabilities

  • Deposits
  • Borrowings
  • Equity

AP FRQs frequently ask you to update a bank's T-account after deposits, withdrawals, or new loans.

Money Multiplier Mechanics

  • Each new loan becomes a deposit elsewhere, expanding M1.
  • Lower RR → more lending → larger multiplier.
  • Banks may hold precautionary reserves, shrinking the actual multiplier relative to the simple formula.

Leakages that Reduce the Multiplier

  • Cash Drain: Public holds more currency, leaving fewer deposits to multiply.
  • Excess Reserves: Banks sit on liquidity instead of lending.

Ample vs Limited Reserves Regimes

  • Ample reserves: Banks already hold excess cash; changes in RR/ER affect behavior, but interest on reserves becomes the primary tool.
  • Limited reserves: The Fed must supply or drain reserves to hit interest-rate targets; small changes move market rates.
