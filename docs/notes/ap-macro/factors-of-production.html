Chapter 2 · Factors of Production & Opportunity Cost

Everything produced in the economy begins with four basic resources. Recognizing their limits explains why opportunity costs rise as we devote more to any single good.

The Four Factors

Land

All natural resources: land, water, minerals, timber, energy deposits. Payments take the form of rent.

Labor

Human effort, both physical and mental. Includes skills (human capital). Paid through wages.

Capital

Man-made tools, machinery, factories, and technology used to produce other goods. Earning is interest.

Entrepreneurship

Risk-taking and innovation that combine other factors into new products or processes. Reward is profit.

Choice Requires Sacrifice

  • Resources are finite → producing more of one good means producing less of another.
  • Trade-offs: Each decision compares the value of the chosen option to the value of the best alternative.
  • Opportunity cost (OC): The value of the next-best alternative foregone.

Example: Holding cash instead of a bond with 4% annual interest creates an opportunity cost of 4% × amount of cash held.

Why Opportunity Cost Rises

Resources are not perfectly adaptable. As we pull land, labor, or capital away from their best use, productivity falls and OC climbs.

  • Law of Increasing Opportunity Cost: Producing additional units of a good requires larger and larger sacrifices of the other good.
  • Reason: Specialized resources—some are better suited for good A, others for good B.
  • Implication: The production possibilities frontier (PPF) bows outward.

Scarcity Applied: Monthly Labor Hours

If an economy has 50,000 labor hours available per month, those hours must be allocated between competing industries. Moving 5,000 hours from health care into consumer electronics raises gadget output but reduces medical services—an explicit opportunity cost.
